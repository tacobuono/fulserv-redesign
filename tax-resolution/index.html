IRS tax resolution and audit defense in New York

When a tax authority wants something from you, you need a CPA to work the numbers and an attorney to protect your rights. At Fulserv they're the same person, backed by a team of ten in Midtown Manhattan.

What we handle

Audits

Correspondence, office and field audits. We gather the records, answer the requests and negotiate the findings, so you are not facing the examiner alone.

Notices and balances due

We check what's owed before you pay it, correct errors, and respond on time so a small notice doesn't grow into a collection case.

Collections, levies and liens

We work out payment arrangements, settlements or hardship status that fit what you can pay, and press for release of levies already in place.

Penalty relief

Reasonable-cause and first-time abatement requests can take penalties off the balance. We make the case in writing.

Appeals

When an examiner gets it wrong, we take the case to the IRS Independent Office of Appeals with a written protest built on the facts and the law.

Tax Court and litigation

For disputes that don't settle, we petition the U.S. Tax Court and represent you through settlement or trial.

Common IRS letters, in plain English

The notice or letter number sits in the top or bottom right corner. The date printed on your notice is the deadline that counts.

CP2000

The IRS thinks income is missing from your return.

The IRS compared your return with the W-2s and 1099s that employers, banks and brokers sent in, and the numbers don't match. This is a proposal, not a final bill. You can agree, partly agree, or dispute it.

Respond by: Usually 30 days from the notice date

CP14

The IRS says you have a balance due.

This is the first bill for tax the IRS says hasn't been paid. Interest and penalties keep adding up until the balance is paid or an arrangement is in place.

Respond by: The pay-by date on the notice, often about three weeks out

CP504

The IRS intends to levy if the balance stays unpaid.

Earlier balance-due notices went unanswered. The IRS can now take your state tax refund, and it is one notice away from levying wages and bank accounts.

Respond by: The date printed on the notice

LT11 or Letter 1058

Final notice before levy, and your right to a hearing.

This is the last notice before the IRS can levy wages, bank accounts and other property. It also gives you the right to a Collection Due Process hearing with the IRS Independent Office of Appeals.

Respond by: 30 days from the letter date to request a hearing

Letter 566 or 2205

Your return has been selected for audit.

The IRS wants to examine your return, either by mail or at a meeting. Audits are narrower than most people fear, but what you hand over and how you answer shapes the result.

Respond by: The response or appointment date in the letter

Letter 3219

Notice of deficiency: the IRS has decided you owe more.

This letter is your ticket to the U.S. Tax Court, where you can dispute the amount before paying it. Miss the deadline and that option is gone.

Respond by: 90 days to petition the Tax Court (150 if mailed to you outside the U.S.). It cannot be extended.

How a case runs

  1. Consultation and assessment

    We start with a detailed conversation about your dispute, gather the notices and documents, and review your returns and IRS records. By the end you know where you stand.

  2. Strategy

    We work through the law, regulations and precedent that apply to your facts, find where there is room to negotiate, and agree on a plan with you.

  3. Negotiation and representation

    We handle all communication with the tax authority, present the evidence and arguments, and press for the outcome that fits your goals.

  4. Resolution and compliance

    We explain the terms of the result, make sure every obligation is met, and help you stay compliant so the dispute doesn't repeat.

Questions people ask first

Do I have to talk to the IRS myself?

Usually not. Once you sign a power of attorney (Form 2848), the IRS deals with us, and most calls and meetings can be left to us.

What if I can't pay what I owe?

There are options: installment agreements, an offer in compromise in some cases, and hardship status when paying would leave you unable to cover basic living expenses. Which one fits depends on your income, assets and history.

Do you work with clients outside New York City?

Yes. We serve clients across the New York metropolitan area, including New Jersey.

How fast do I need to act?

Before the date printed on your notice. Some deadlines, like the 90 days to petition the Tax Court, can't be extended, and missing them gives up rights you can't get back.
