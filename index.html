Got a letter? Find the code in its top or bottom right corner.

Notice
CP2000

The IRS thinks income is missing from your return.

The IRS compared your return with the W-2s and 1099s that employers, banks and brokers sent in, and the numbers don't match. This is a proposal, not a final bill. You can agree, partly agree, or dispute it.

Respond by Usually 30 days from the notice date

What we do

  • Match every W-2 and 1099 against your records to find the gap
  • Write the response, with documents that support your side
  • Ask for penalty relief where the rules allow it

Notice
CP14

The IRS says you have a balance due.

This is the first bill for tax the IRS says hasn't been paid. Interest and penalties keep adding up until the balance is paid or an arrangement is in place.

Respond by The pay-by date on the notice, often about three weeks out

What we do

  • Check that the balance is right before you pay it
  • Request penalty relief, including first-time abatement if you qualify
  • Set up a payment arrangement you can keep
Get help with this letter
Notice
CP504

The IRS intends to levy if the balance stays unpaid.

Earlier balance-due notices went unanswered. The IRS can now take your state tax refund, and it is one notice away from levying wages and bank accounts.

Respond by The date printed on the notice

What we do

  • Contact the IRS for you to stop the escalation
  • Compare payment plans, settlement and hardship status against your finances
  • Keep your right to a hearing intact
Get help with this letter
Notice
LT11 or Letter 1058

Final notice before levy, and your right to a hearing.

This is the last notice before the IRS can levy wages, bank accounts and other property. It also gives you the right to a Collection Due Process hearing with the IRS Independent Office of Appeals.

Respond by 30 days from the letter date to request a hearing

What we do

  • File the hearing request (Form 12153) before the 30 days run out
  • Present collection alternatives to Appeals
  • Push for release of any levy already in place
Get help with this letter
Letter
566 or 2205

Your return has been selected for audit.

The IRS wants to examine your return, either by mail or at a meeting. Audits are narrower than most people fear, but what you hand over and how you answer shapes the result.

Respond by The response or appointment date in the letter

What we do

  • File a power of attorney (Form 2848) so the IRS deals with us, not you
  • Organize records and answer document requests
  • Attend meetings and contest adjustments we disagree with
Get help with this letter
Letter
3219

Notice of deficiency: the IRS has decided you owe more.

This letter is your ticket to the U.S. Tax Court, where you can dispute the amount before paying it. Miss the deadline and that option is gone.

Respond by 90 days to petition the Tax Court (150 if mailed to you outside the U.S.). It cannot be extended.

What we do

  • File the Tax Court petition before the deadline
  • Negotiate a settlement with IRS Appeals and Counsel
  • Represent you through settlement or trial
