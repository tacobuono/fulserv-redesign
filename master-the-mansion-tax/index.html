Master the Mansion Tax: Think Twice, Get Advice

As a small business owner, one of your primary concerns is managing your finances and taxes well. Whether you deal with income tax, payroll tax or property tax, knowing your obligations is crucial. One tax creating a lot of buzz in New York City and New Jersey is the mansion tax.

What is the mansion tax?

The mansion tax is a tax imposed on residential properties sold above a certain value in New York or New Jersey. When it was introduced in 1989, it was a flat 1% on all residential properties sold for $1 million or more.

In July 2019, it changed significantly. The mansion tax is now progressive, meaning the rate varies with the sale price. It starts at 1% for properties sold between $1 million and $2 million, and rises to 3.9% for properties sold for $25 million or more.

What does this tax mean for business owners?

The direct impact of the mansion tax falls on residential property buyers. It can still reach some small business owners, particularly those who own their business premises or are considering buying property in NYC or NJ.

If you plan to buy residential property to convert into a workspace, or as an investment, factor the mansion tax into your costs. The higher rates on high-priced properties can significantly affect your return.

And if you plan to sell residential property you currently use for your business, the mansion tax may shrink the pool of buyers. Some will be reluctant to pay the additional tax on a high-priced property.

Being aware of and understanding tax laws can help you make informed decisions that positively impact the financial health of your small business.

Tips for planning around the mansion tax

  • Plan ahead. If you are buying or selling property, build the mansion tax into your calculations. It helps you set a realistic budget or asking price.
  • Consult a tax professional. Tax laws are complex and always changing. A professional can explain how the mansion tax applies to you and how to limit its impact.
  • Diversify your investments. If you invest in property, avoid putting everything into high-value properties that attract the heaviest mansion tax.

In conclusion

The mansion tax mainly concerns property buyers and sellers in NYC and NJ, but it can reach small business owners too. Understanding it helps you make decisions that protect the financial health of your business.
